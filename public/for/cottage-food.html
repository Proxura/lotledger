Lot numbers and batch records for cottage food businesses

Cottage food laws let you sell from a home kitchen. Most of them also expect you to keep production records: what you made, when, how much, and what went in. LotLedger is that record, kept on your phone instead of in a binder.

A lot number on every batch, assigned for you

Pick a prefix once. Each batch gets the next number. Write it on the label; the app keeps the rest.

Ingredients and suppliers per batch

Flour from which mill, which sack, which date. Sugar, pectin, oil, whatever you use. Free text, as specific as you want.

Units made, units out, units on hand

Markets, online orders, samples, waste. Each is a tap against the lot. The count is always current and you never show a customer a number you cannot back up.

Records you can hand over

When a program inspector or a customer with a complaint asks, export the ledger as one spreadsheet. Every lot, every movement, dated.

What a lot looks like

Michigan is one example. Its Cottage Food Law puts the product name, the ingredients, allergens, the producer's name and address and the home-kitchen statement on every label, and requires you to keep sales records and show them to an MDARD inspector on request. A lot number is not required on the label, but it is the only thing that ties the jar in a customer's hand to the record you kept.
